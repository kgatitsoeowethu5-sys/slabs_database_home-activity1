**PART 1: Explanation of each management level**

A. Operational level

- Management Level: Operational
- Corresponding Information System: Transaction Processing System (TPS)
- Data Flow & Purpose: This level handles daily business activities and records detailed, routine transactions, such as sales, customer payments, stock movements, and employee attendance. The information helps supervisors monitor daily operations, maintain accurate records, and ensure that work runs smoothly.

Example: A supermarket's TPS records every item sold at the checkout and updates inventory records.



B. Tactical level

- Management Level: Tactical
- Corresponding Information System: Management Information System (MIS) and Decision Support System (DSS)
- Data Flow & Purpose: Middle managers use summarized operational data, reports, and analytical information to monitor performance, allocate resources, prepare budgets, and solve business problems. MIS provides regular reports, while DSS helps managers compare alternatives and make decisions.

Example: A supermarket manager reviews weekly sales reports and uses a DSS to decide whether to offer discounts on products with declining sales.




C. Strategic level

- Management Level: Strategic
- Corresponding Information System: Executive Support System (ESS)
- Data Flow & Purpose: Senior executives use summarized internal information and external data, such as market trends, competitor performance, and economic forecasts. This information supports long-term planning, policy development, and major business decisions.

Example: A supermarket CEO reviews annual sales trends and competitor information to decide whether the company should open new branches.


**How information flows through the organization**

1. Operational to Tactical: Daily transactions are collected and summarized into reports for middle managers.

2. Tactical to Strategic: Summarized reports and business analyses help executives identify trends and make long-term decisions.

3. Strategic to Tactical: Executives communicate business goals and strategies to middle managers.

4. Tactical to Operational: Middle managers translate strategies into budgets, targets, schedules, and instructions for operational employees.
Conclusion: The three management levels work together through information systems. TPS supports daily operations, MIS and DSS support middle-management decisions, and ESS supports long-term strategic planning. Information flows upward to support decision-making and downward to guide organizational activities.



**Part 2: Technical Architecture Definitions**

1. IT Infrastructure Diagram

An IT Infrastructure Diagram shows the physical technology assets used by an organization. These include computers, servers, routers, switches, cables, and other network equipment. It illustrates how these physical devices are connected and how they communicate with each other. This diagram helps IT staff manage hardware, troubleshoot network problems, and plan equipment upgrades.


2. IT Landscape Diagram

An IT Landscape Diagram shows the different types of IT assets and systems an organization uses at a logical level. These include software applications, databases, operating systems, cloud services, and business systems. Unlike an infrastructure diagram, it focuses on what systems exist and how they relate to one another rather than the physical hardware connections. It helps management identify duplicate systems, outdated technology, and opportunities to reduce IT costs.


3. IT Architecture Diagram

An IT Architecture Diagram shows the overall structure and design principles that guide an organization's technology systems. It outlines the relationships between business processes, applications, data, and technology components. It also defines the concepts, rules, norms, and standards that systems should follow to ensure security, compatibility, reliability, and consistency. This diagram helps an organization plan future technology improvements and ensure that its IT systems support its business goals.
